---
title: "Access Removal"
description: "How access is safely removed when a volunteer leaves."
---

When a volunteer leaves, every grant of access they held is removed promptly and completely. Access without a person behind it is a standing risk, so this procedure is deliberate and checklist-driven.

## The checklist

- [ ] Remove the volunteer from all organization and admin accounts
- [ ] Remove repository and infrastructure access granted via [Server Access](/handbook/tech-protocols/server-access)
- [ ] Rotate or invalidate any credentials they had access to
- [ ] Review open sessions and revoke tokens where applicable
- [ ] Update communication channels and contact records

## Order of operations

The sequence runs from most-privileged to least-privileged, and each step is performed by management (or the system's maintainer where one exists), with completion verified before the next begins.

Verification is simple and deliberate: attempt the access as the departing volunteer would, and confirm it fails. An offboarding that was never tested is a checklist that was never done.

## Departing volunteer responsibilities

A departing volunteer is asked to:

- Hand over or clearly document any in-flight work, so nothing depends on knowledge that's leaving with them.
- Confirm that Rigiity data on their personal machines has been returned or destroyed, per their own judgment and our trust in them. We don't conduct forensic audits of departing volunteers; the relationship is built on trust, and the offboarding preserves that tone.
- Know that leaving in good standing leaves the door open: volunteers return, and offboarding is not a punishment ritual.
