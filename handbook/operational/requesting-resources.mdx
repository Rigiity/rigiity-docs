---
title: "Requesting Resources"
description: "How volunteers request access, tools, and budget."
---

Volunteers need things to do their work: repository access, accounts on platforms, system access, or occasionally budget for a project need. Requests are deliberate, so access and spending always have a recorded reason.

## The flow

Resources follow approval. The path is:

1. **The work is agreed first.** For a new project, that means an approved [proposal](/handbook/operational/proposing-a-project). For work inside an existing project, it means the maintainer or management agrees the task is real. Access is granted for work that exists, not in anticipation of work that might.
2. **The request goes to the right channel.** There is no single form: the channel depends on what's being requested.
   - **Repository access**: handled directly through repository permissions. A maintainer grants the role that matches the work, and the volunteer verifies their access works. That's the whole process, and it's deliberately simple.
   - **System or sensitive access**: requested through [security@rigiity.com](mailto:security@rigiity.com) or the internal chat, whichever fits the context, and granted per [Server Access](/handbook/tech-protocols/server-access).
   - **Other platforms**: each platform has its own way of verifying and granting access, and the request follows that platform's process.
   - **Budget or spending**: requested through management directly, stated with the reason and the amount.
3. **The grant is recorded where the work lives.** A comment on the task or proposal, so the reason for the access is as permanent as the access itself.

## External contributors

For public repositories, contributing usually requires no permissions at all: an external contributor opens a pull request with signed commits, and it goes through the normal review and merge process. The merge is the access. Proprietary repositories are where the general steps above apply.

## What reviewers weigh

- Whether the request matches work that's actually agreed and scoped.
- Whether the level of access is the minimum that does the job.
- For anything new the org would run or depend on: the [security audit requirement](/handbook/tech-protocols/security-guidelines#evaluating-new-dependencies-and-tools) applies before adoption, not after.
