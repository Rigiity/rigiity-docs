---
title: "Enforcement stance"
description: "How Rigiity responds when its work is misused."
---

Our objective in any dispute is getting infringing content down, not collecting money. Enforcement is proportionate, calm, and documented; we won't burn resources on symbolic battles over small things, and we won't let ourselves be walked over either.

## If someone violates a license of our work

If a violation of a license on something we've released ever comes up, the approach is:

1. A peaceful ask: contact the violator, explain the license, and ask them to come into compliance. Most reasonable people fix it when asked.
2. If they don't listen and the violation actually matters, report it to their hosting platform.
3. Legal action is a last resort we would consider only for a serious violation worth the effort.

We're not in the business of policing people. Trivial or unfixable cases get ignored; being stuck-up about licensing helps nobody.

## What we don't do

We don't send hostile letters over trivia, we don't pursue people across jurisdictions we can't reach, and we don't treat every copy as an attack. Good-faith reuse under our licenses is the point of the licenses.
