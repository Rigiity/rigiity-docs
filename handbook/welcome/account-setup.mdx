This page walks a new volunteer through getting plugged in. It's written generically because every volunteer's setup depends on what they want to work on.

## Before you begin

Volunteering at Rigiity starts with a conversation, not an account. Reach out through [our contact page](https://rigiity.com/contact/) or any of our presences listed on [rigiity.com/presences](https://rigiity.com/presences/). We get to know every prospective volunteer before granting access; it keeps the team trusted and accountable to each other.

Once you're approved, the setup below applies.

## Accounts you may need

Depending on your area of work:

- **GitHub account**: for repositories hosted on GitHub. Most of our code and this documentation live there.
- **GitLab account**: for repositories hosted on GitLab.
- **Email**: Rigiity management operates `@rigiity.com` email addresses, and that's the reliable channel for anything official.

You don't need all of these on day one. Set up what your first tasks require, and ask if you're unsure.

## Securing your accounts

Before anything else, secure what you've created per the [Security Guidelines](/handbook/tech-protocols/security-guidelines): multi-factor authentication everywhere it's offered, strong unique passphrases in a password manager, and signing keys for Git commits set up and passphrase-protected. Doing this first means access can be granted to an account that's already protected, rather than protecting an account that already has access.

## What you can access by default

New volunteers start with access to standard communication channels only: the internal chat, and the public spaces anyone can see. Everything else (repositories, systems, tools) is granted explicitly based on the area you want to work in, per [Requesting Resources](/handbook/operational/requesting-resources).

The sequence to expect: agree on what you'll work on first, then request what that work needs. Access follows the work, not the other way around.

## If something goes wrong

Try the obvious fixes first: re-authenticating, checking you're using the right account, and confirming your access was actually granted. If that doesn't resolve it, search for the error: copy the exact message into a search engine, since most tooling problems have been asked and answered somewhere already. If the search doesn't turn up anything usable, email management and include what you were trying to do, what you expected, what happened instead, and what you already tried. That's everything needed to fix most setup problems in one round trip.
