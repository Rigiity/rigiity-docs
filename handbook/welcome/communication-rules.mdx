Clear, kind, direct communication is part of our quality standard. These rules apply everywhere Rigiity volunteers interact.

## Internal communication

We keep internal communication light. There are three kinds of channels, each with a clear job:

- **Project work happens in the repository**: issues for tasks and bugs, pull requests for changes and their reviews. If a discussion changes what gets built, it belongs there, where it's public and permanent.
- **Quick, informal coordination happens in our private chat.** Decisions that matter get copied into the repository so they don't stay buried in chat scrollback.
- **Anything official goes through `@rigiity.com` email**: access requests, approvals, security reports, and anything that needs a record. Security issues go to [security@rigiity.com](mailto:security@rigiity.com) per [our security page](https://rigiity.com/security/).

The pattern to remember: chat is for conversation, repositories are for decisions, email is for records.

**Response expectations**: there are none, formally. Rigiity is volunteer-built in free time, and the [pace and availability](/handbook/welcome/overview#pace-and-availability) principles apply inside the org too. If something is genuinely urgent, say so and say why; otherwise assume people will get to it.

## Public communication

### Representing Rigiity

Volunteers are honest about Rigiity, including its flaws. Criticism is welcome; bad faith is not. Official statements (announcements, releases, responses made on behalf of the organization) come from management, whether posted directly or through an official account on our presences that management operates.

### What volunteers post on their own

Volunteers speak for themselves. What you post on your own accounts is yours to decide, the same as anywhere else on the internet, with a few exceptions that exist to protect the organization and the people in it:

- **Unreleased work**: projects that are neither open-source nor in beta stay behind the scenes until they ship, and it works best if volunteers keep them there too. Open-source and beta projects are yours to discuss freely, within the other exceptions on this list.
- **Confidential material**: credentials, internal discussions, private communications, and anything a reasonable person would understand to be shared in confidence.
- **Other people's privacy**: personal information about volunteers or community members that they have not made public themselves.
- **The official voice**: presenting your personal post as an official Rigiity statement, or speaking on behalf of the organization without being asked to.
- **Bad faith**: the same standard as everywhere else in this handbook. Criticism is welcome; fabrication, harassment, and malice are not.

When in doubt about whether something crosses a line, ask management before posting. That question costs minutes; a leak costs trust. For how we reference other companies' names and brands, see [Mentioning other companies](/handbook/legal/mentioning-other-companies).

### We let finished work do the talking

We don't like marketing things early. Announcing an unfinished project creates expectations, and managing expectations is a distraction from finishing the project. So we tend to work quietly, sometimes for months, and there may be nothing to see until the day something releases.

That's just how we prefer to build, not a rule we impose on anyone. And it's not a sign that we've disappeared: when something is ready, it appears, complete, and it will have been worth the quiet.

## Conduct

The standards are simple and match how we communicate everywhere: be honest, be kind, argue in good faith, and treat volunteers and community members as people.

### When someone falls short

Most problems get solved by talking. If someone's behavior is out of line, the first step is always a direct, good-faith conversation with them. Escalation exists for the rare case where talking doesn't work: management may remove someone from Rigiity spaces, and in serious cases from the organization itself. Removal is a last resort, not a first response.

### Personal spaces

Volunteers' personal accounts and spaces are their own. Rigiity's conduct standards apply there only when the situation genuinely involves the organization: for example, using a personal space to harass someone over Rigiity business, or leaking confidential material. What volunteers do entirely on their own time, about their own topics, is their own business.

When someone drags Rigiity into a dispute it has no part in, our standing response is simple: Rigiity does not comment on unrelated conflicts, and we ask everyone involved to leave the organization out of it. We don't take sides in other people's drama, and we expect the same courtesy.

### Hard lines

A few things are not conduct violations to be talked through; they are immediate grounds for removal, whatever the circumstances:

- Harassment, threats, or doxxing of anyone.
- Deliberate deception: fabricating work, impersonating others, or presenting bad-faith claims as fact.
- Leaking credentials, private communications, or unreleased work that was shared in confidence.

For how we respond when our work is misused, see [Enforcement stance](/handbook/legal/enforcement-stance).
